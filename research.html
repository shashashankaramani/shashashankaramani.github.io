Deep Enteroscopy Medical Device Design

Undergraduate Research · MIT

Sep 2026 – Present

Developing a deployable mechanical mechanism for deep enteroscopy with the goal of improving procedural safety and enabling rapid withdrawal in the event of complications while preserving the benefits of motorized spiral enteroscopy.

Overview of enteroscopy and endoscopic access to the small intestine. Image: Aliveo Mdical.

Clinical Motivation

Deep enteroscopy enables access to regions of the small intestine that are difficult to reach using conventional endoscopic methods. My work focuses on developing a mechanical fail-safe concept that could allow controlled withdrawal of the device in the event of procedural complications.

Biomedical Devices Deep Enteroscopy Mechanical Safety Assistive Technology

Mechanism Design

Designed a deployable tubular mechanism using patterned slit geometries that transform under compression. The concept uses spiral and vertical slit patterns to create controlled radial deployment while maintaining a compact undeployed form.

Evaluating flexible PVC tubing materials and wall thicknesses to understand how geometry and material properties affect deformation, deployment behavior, and mechanical response.

Vision-Based Equine Health Monitoring

MIT Sports Lab · Undergraduate Research Fellow

January 2026 – May 2026
Cambridge, Massachusetts

Developed computer vision and machine-learning tools for early identification of equine health abnormalities from video data, while contributing across the full workflow from data collection and labeling to model training, validation, and deployment.

Example horse detection and monitoring frame used in the computer vision pipeline.

Computer Vision & Model Development

Worked across the full machine-learning pipeline for equine health monitoring, including video data labeling, dataset preparation, model training, and validation. Developed an optical flow-based computer vision pipeline to analyze horse movement and used vision-language models to support large-scale video analysis.

Evaluated model behavior during training and validation by reviewing prediction quality, loss trends, and failure cases, gaining experience with both the data and model-development sides of the research process.
